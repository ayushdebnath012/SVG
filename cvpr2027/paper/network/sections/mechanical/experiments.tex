\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Primary two-source adaptation}
A fresh 3B backbone uses rank-16 adapters with alpha 320 on seven attention/MLP projections in the last twelve transformer blocks. Seed 17, learning rate $10^{-4}$, microbatch one and four accumulation steps give effective batch four. Two fixed epochs on a Colab T4 complete 1,270 optimizer updates in 20,923.7 seconds. Mean completion loss is 0.2495. The saved manifest records \texttt{torch.bfloat16}; native BF16 hardware acceleration is not established. We reload the final adapter, check all 168 saved tensors for finite values and evaluate both fresh base and trained model greedily on identical held-out inputs. The 9,977,856 adapter scalars and artifact hashes are saved.

\paragraph{Executable is not necessarily correct.}
Table~\ref{tab:primary} separates interface success from shape agreement. Fine-tuning increases applicability from 39 to 173 tasks and execution from 7 to 139. Strict agreement rises from 1/252 to 39/252 (15.48\,\%), with 25/124 BenchCAD and 14/128 CAD-Editor matches. Only 39/139 executed outputs (28.06\,\%) reach the strict threshold, leaving considerable geometric error. Broad agreement reaches 52/252. Exact target program/sequence and patch agreement are 32 and 31 cases, respectively, versus one each for the base. No final generation reaches the output cap.

Six reference errors and one whole-worker timeout remain in the full denominator. The scorer's reference-valid bookkeeping differs between base and final outputs (246 versus 245); 245 is therefore not treated as a fixed valid-reference test set. These counts measure released-target agreement, not audited compliance with every instruction.

\paragraph{What this comparison establishes.}
The same-model base/final comparison supports a local adaptation gain under fixed prompts and decoding. It is not a matched comparison with CAD-Editor or other published editing methods. One seed, synthetic online supervision, token filtering and unestablished cross-source ancestry limit generalization. Earlier 1.5B Apple and H100 runs use different data/settings and are preserved as separate supplementary experiments (Appendix~\ref{sec:earlier-experiments}); their counts are not pooled with the primary run.

\paragraph{Frontier model on the same holdout.}
We evaluate GPT-6 Astra~\cite{openai2026astra} zero-shot on all 252 held-out tasks with the identical system prompt, input, patch contract and scorer (medium reasoning effort, one sample per task, \$\paAstraCost{} at list price). It reaches \paAstra/252 strict matches (\paAstraBench{} BenchCAD, \paAstraEditor{} CAD-Editor), against \paSft{} for the final 3B adapter. On paired tasks, only Astra succeeds on \paAstraOnly{} and only the adapter on \paSftOnlyVsAstra{} (exact McNemar $p=\paAstraVsSftP$). The gap lies in BenchCAD programs: on CAD-Editor sequences the two are statistically indistinguishable (\paAstraEditorOnly{} versus \paSftEditorOnly{} discordant tasks, $p=\paAstraVsSftEditorP$), where \paAstraEditorWrong{} frontier answers execute but disagree with the reconstructed target and \paAstraEditorInvalid{} violate the patch or six-bit command contract. Some frontier misses follow the instruction more literally than the released target, \eg a requested 21\,mm through-hole against a released 20.98\,mm hole; strict agreement measures target reproduction, not instruction compliance. One execution-feedback repair round, in which Astra sees its failed answer and the validator or executor error but no reference, makes \paRepairValid{} of its \paRepairTasks{} failed patches valid and \paRepairExec{} executable, yet adds only \paRepairStrict{} strict match (low reasoning effort, \$\paRepairCost).

\paragraph{GRPO-trained verifier for Astra.}
We also train a separate verifier policy with GRPO~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath}: a Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B LoRA policy chooses among four constrained actions (execute the candidate, check instruction numbers, accept, reject) and must observe at least one tool result before a verdict. It learns from \agentTrain{} saved Astra candidates on BenchCAD training tasks, with \agentDev{} component-disjoint development candidates, over \agentGroups{} groups of \agentGroupSize{} trajectories; reference agreement defines the reward but never enters its observations. Each held-out Astra answer it rejects receives one repair request, and a repair replaces the original only if the policy accepts it. On the \agentN{} BenchCAD held-out tasks, this raises Astra from \agentBefore{} to \agentAfter{} strict matches (\agentRecovered{} recovered, \agentRegressed{} regressed, $p=\agentP$; \agentRepairs{} repair calls, \$\agentCost). The verifier itself is weak: its held-out verdict accuracy (\agentAcc\,\%) is below always accepting Astra's answer (\agentAlways\,\%), and on development it matches that baseline (\agentDevAcc{} versus \agentDevAlways\,\%). The small gain is therefore better attributed to the extra repair calls than to learned verification.

\paragraph{Plan-then-patch with quote anchoring.}
We retrain the same 3B recipe with each answer formatted as a short plan followed by the unchanged patch. One plan bullet per operation states the zero-based original index, quotes that original line and names the action. Template plans are generated deterministically from reference patches, so this format adds no supervision beyond the patch. Decoded verbatim, the plan model is no better than the patch-only adapter (\paTemplateADecoded{} and \paTemplateBDecoded{} strict in two training runs). The first run's \paIndexFailures{} out-of-range BenchCAD patches share one failure: the plan quotes the correct source line but miscounts its index, \eg the intended cut line labelled 21 in a 19-line program. Quote anchoring therefore moves each operation to the unique source line equal to its quoted text and otherwise keeps the predicted index; it reads only the source program and the model output. Anchoring raises both runs to \paTemplateAAnchored/252 (\paTemplateAAnchorGain{} and \paTemplateBAnchorGain{} tasks gained, none lost), against \paSft{} for the patch-only adapter (first run: \paTemplateAVsSftGain{} gained, \paTemplateAVsSftLoss{} lost, $p=\paTemplateAVsSftP$). Because the rule was designed from first-run test errors, we confirm it on the 172 validation tasks of the second run, which played no role in its design: strict agreement rises from \paValDecoded{} to \paValAnchored{} (\paValGain{} gained, \paValLoss{} lost, $p=\paValP$). The two runs disagree on \paRunChurnGain{}+\paRunChurnLoss{} tasks with equal totals, so single-run differences of a few tasks are not meaningful.

\paragraph{Verifier-selected sampling.}
Unlike Astra, the plan student produces diverse answers. Besides its greedy answer we sample \bonSamples{} answers per task at temperature 1.0 and anchor each, giving \bonDistinct{} distinct programs per test task on average. A target-free verifier selects one candidate from the source program, instruction and candidates alone: a candidate must apply and execute to a valid solid, values requested in the instruction should appear in its inserted lines, and candidates are clustered by executed geometry (volume IoU $\geq0.99999$), preferring the largest agreeing cluster. If no candidate executes, the greedy answer is kept. Selection raises strict agreement from \bonGreedy{} to \bonPick/252 on test (\bonGain{} gained, \bonLoss{} lost, $p=\bonP$) and from \bonValGreedy{} to \bonValPick{} on validation (\bonValGain{} gained, \bonValLoss{} lost, $p=\bonValP$); majority vote reaches \bonMajority{}, and always choosing a correct candidate when one exists would reach \bonOracle{}. On CAD-Editor sequences the selected student exceeds Astra (\bonEditorPick{} versus \bonEditorAstra{}; \bonEditorOursOnly{} versus \bonEditorAstraOnly{} discordant tasks, $p=\bonEditorP$). The same selection barely helps Astra: on \teacherN{} BenchCAD training tasks with two Astra samples each, choosing between them changes strict agreement from \teacherFirst{} to \teacherPick{} (at most \teacherOracle{}), because its samples rarely differ. The sampled student is a plan model whose \paTeacherAccepted{} BenchCAD training targets are verifier-selected Astra answers; this substitution leaves its greedy agreement equal to template training (\paDistillAnchored{} versus \paTemplateAAnchored{}).

\paragraph{Execution-feedback repair for the student.}
We also let the plan student repair its own failures in a ReAct-style loop. An answer that fails to apply or execute is returned with the task, the failed answer and the validator or executor error, and the student regenerates greedily, for up to \reactRounds{} rounds. Across the \reactTasks{} test and validation tasks, answers passing both checks rise from \reactGatesA{} to \reactGatesB{} after one round and do not change afterwards. Strict agreement moves from \bonGreedy{} to \reactFinal{} on test (\reactGain{} gained, \reactLoss{} lost) and stays at \reactValFinal{} on validation. Without repair-specific training, the student rarely turns an error message into a correct edit; sampling with verifier selection is far more effective.

\paragraph{Difficulty tiers.}
We group held-out tasks by the reasoning an instruction requires, independently of model outputs (Table~\ref{tab:tiers}); per-category results were known when the grouping was fixed, so it is descriptive rather than preregistered. On easy edits the selected student is statistically indistinguishable from Astra (\tierEasyBest{} versus \tierEasyAstra{}, $p=\tierEasyP$). The gap lies in moderate edits that need derived values or feature changes (\tierModerateBest{} versus \tierModerateAstra{}, $p=\tierModerateP$), while hard edits remain largely unsolved by every system (at most \tierHardAstra/\tierHardN). Shape complexity does not order difficulty: on equal-size tertiles of source-solid face count, Astra reaches \shapeEasyAstra, \shapeModerateAstra{} and \shapeHardAstra\,\%, and the selected student \shapeEasyBest, \shapeModerateBest{} and \shapeHardBest\,\%, from fewest to most faces.

\subsection{Focused multi-view edit audit}

\label{fig:edit-localization}

The post-hoc audit uses the fixed six previously illustrated held-out cases, selected before this visual measurement. All six older H100 solids are available; only four completed final 3B outputs are eligible. The 3B propeller patch fails syntax and the screw pipeline is incomplete. We do not assign them projected geometry scores or treat four available cases as six successes.

Figure~\ref{fig:edit-localization} examines a functional flange edit. For the older prediction, top-view silhouette IoU is 0.908 but source-relative edit-mask IoU is 0.516. The final 3B output is 1.000 for both at three-decimal precision. Averaging whole silhouettes across views would further obscure a localized hole-pattern error. This supports examining the requested changed region alongside bulk volume and overall projected similarity; it does not establish general feature detection accuracy.

Four-view records, STEP hashes and 512/1,024-pixel sensitivity are provided in Appendix~\ref{sec:visual-audit}. One-pixel reference-change masks in the flange isometric view illustrate an important limitation: a nearly perfect solid can receive a poor edit-mask score in a view with effectively no visible change. We therefore report the top view and raw denominators, and treat aggregate projected edit scores as diagnostic rather than a certification.

\subsection{Mechanical examples with response checks}
We attempt the same six illustrative instructions with unmodified final 3B patches. Mounting angle, locator block, shaft and flange complete strict geometry, numerical, mesh-volume and target-compliance checks. Their compliance discrepancies are 0.001123\,\%, 0.046691\,\%, 0.009141\,\% and 0.013270\,\%, respectively. The invalid propeller and incomplete screw attempts remain unverified; the screw has no saved original FEM verdict establishing its cause. Figure~\ref{fig:fem-large-method} shows saved meshes, displacement and stress fields for two completed functional edits. This subset establishes workflow feasibility under diagnostic contracts, not full-benchmark FEM coverage or real operating safety.
